Fadi
Elchamaa

Lebanese painter
Lives and works in Beirut

His paintings are full of light, with a playful disregard for convention.

My paintings are real. Their surfaces go through rough times. They are layered, scumbled, buried, excavated, scraped, scratched and wiped until light becomes flesh. My paintings are more real than me. (f.elchamaa)

Fadi Bassam Elchamaa (b. 1960, Beirut) is a Lebanese painter who lives and works in Beirut. Born into a Damascene family, he began painting in the early 1980s.

In 1995, Elchamaa presented Flowers on the Floor at the Forum Art Show in Beirut. The fourteen-metre-long painting was laid on the floor beneath Plexiglas so visitors could walk over it. That year he also exhibited at Yarzeh Art Gallery in Mount Lebanon and took part in the Sursock Museum’s Salon d’Automne, where he exhibited again in 1996. In 1997, he presented Apples at Rochane Gallery in Beirut.

That same year, Elchamaa began working in advertising alongside his painting. He lived and worked in Beirut, Damascus, Khobar, Manama, Cairo, Riyadh, Casablanca and Doha. The more he moved between these cities, the more elusive Arab identity felt to him. Transformations was his response to that realisation. At Joanna Seikali Gallery in 2011, he turned thirty large portrait paintings into coloured ropes and suspended them in the gallery. He left advertising at the end of 2013.

In 2019, Elchamaa and artist Semaan Khawam co-founded Sleeping with the Enemy, a project that brought painters into galleries to work in front of visitors. The first two editions took place that year with three artists.

Questions of identity were still central to Elchamaa’s practice when he presented his retrospective, And Tomorrow Who Am I?, at Artlab in October 2019. He invited visitors to curate the exhibition from an archive of about two hundred portraits.

Sleeping with the Enemy returned in 2020 with eleven artists. Later that year, during the pandemic, Elchamaa and Khawam painted head-to-head.

After his 2019 retrospective, Elchamaa began devoting much of his painting to abstract portraiture. He wanted to paint a universal self, a presence free of the stigma of identity. The entities he paints seem familiar and keep changing as you look at them. He works with light as a material. Transparent washes are interrupted by opaque strokes. Colour is rubbed, wiped and sanded away, while hard-edged marks cut through softer passages. Each layer changes what can be seen of the one beneath it. As recognisable forms emerge and dissolve, an innominate presence remains.

His solo exhibitions include Deviations at Galleria Soul in Cairo (2016), Fuqa’a (Bubble) at Galerie Janine Rubeiz (2022), The Disappearing Act at Lynn Modallal Gallery (2023) and Summer Wine at Majaz Gallery (2025). He has also shown work at VOLTA Art Fair in New York and in duo exhibitions in Abidjan and Maryland.

His work is held in the collection of the Ramzi and Saeda Dalloul Art Foundation and in private collections in Lebanon, Côte d’Ivoire, the United States, France and the United Arab Emirates.
