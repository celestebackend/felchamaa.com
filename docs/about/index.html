Fadi
Elchamaa

Lebanese painter
Lives and works in Beirut

His paintings are full of light, with a playful disregard for convention.

Fadi Bassam Elchamaa (b. 1960, Beirut) is a Lebanese painter who lives and works in Beirut. Born into a Damascene family, he began painting in the early 1980s.

In 1997, alongside painting, Elchamaa began a career in advertising. He lived and worked in eight Arab cities: Beirut, Damascus, Khobar, Manama, Cairo, Riyadh, Casablanca and Doha. During these years, he discovered how diverse Arab identity is, and how elusive any single definition can be. That revelation continues to inform his practice. He left advertising at the end of 2013.

Transparent washes are disturbed by opaque strokes; colour is rubbed, wiped and sanded away, while hard-edged marks cut through softer passages. Each layer alters what came before. In this accumulation, he sees something of Arab cultures themselves, shaped by overlapping influences. As recognisable forms dissolve, a sense of presence remains.

Since 2019, Elchamaa has devoted much of his painting to what he calls Innominates: entities that seem familiar but resist identification and refuse to be named.

His practice expands to objects, installations and collaborative projects. In Transformations, presented at Joanna Seikali Gallery in 2011, he transformed thirty large portrait paintings into coloured ropes suspended as an installation. In And Tomorrow Who Am I?, presented at Artlab in 2019, he made an archive of approximately two hundred portraits available to visitors, inviting them to curate the exhibition.

Together with artist Semaan Khawam, he co-founded Sleeping with the Enemy, a project that turns galleries into spaces of public painting, where visitors witness art being made rather than only encounter finished works.

His solo exhibitions include Fuqa’a (Bubble) at Galerie Janine Rubeiz (2022), The Disappearing Act at Lynn Modallal Gallery (2023) and Summer Wine at Majaz Gallery (2025). His work is represented in the collection of the Ramzi and Saeda Dalloul Art Foundation and in private collections in Lebanon, Côte d’Ivoire, the United States, France and the United Arab Emirates.
