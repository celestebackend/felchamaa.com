About Fadi Elchamaa

Fadi Elchamaa

Fadi Bassam Elchamaa (b. 1960, Beirut) is a Lebanese painter who lives and works in Beirut. Born into a Damascene family, he began painting in the early 1980s. His paintings are known for their unconventional use of light and colour, unexpected forms and playful irreverence.

In 1997, alongside painting, Elchamaa began a career in advertising. He lived and worked in eight Arab cities: Beirut, Damascus, Khobar, Manama, Cairo, Riyadh, Casablanca and Doha. During these years, he discovered how diverse Arab identity is and how elusive any single definition can be. That revelation continues to inform his practice. He left advertising at the end of 2013.

Elchamaa uses a wide range of techniques, sometimes combining them on the same surface. Transparent washes are disturbed by opaque strokes; colour is rubbed, wiped and sanded away, while hard-edged marks cut through softer passages. Each layer alters what came before. For Elchamaa, this layering echoes the many influences that have shaped Arab cultures. As recognisable forms dissolve, a sense of presence emerges.

His practice extends to objects, installations and collaborative projects. For Transformations, presented at Joanna Seikali Gallery in 2011, he turned thirty large portrait paintings into coloured ropes and suspended them as an installation. In And Tomorrow Who Am I?, presented at Artlab in 2019, he opened an archive of approximately two hundred portraits, inviting visitors to curate the exhibition.

Together with artist Semaan Khawam, he co-founded Sleeping with the Enemy, a project that turns galleries into spaces of public painting, where visitors can witness art being made rather than only encounter finished works.

His solo exhibitions include Fuqa’a (Bubble) at Galerie Janine Rubeiz (2022), The Disappearing Act at Lynn Modallal Gallery (2023) and Summer Wine at Majaz Gallery (2025).

His work is represented in the collection of the Ramzi and Saeda Dalloul Art Foundation and in private collections in Lebanon, Côte d’Ivoire, the United States, France and the United Arab Emirates.
